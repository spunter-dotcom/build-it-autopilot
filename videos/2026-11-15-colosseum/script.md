How do you seat fifty thousand people in the middle of Rome? The only open ground is an emperor's private lake. So you drain it and dig down to the clay. But soft ground can't carry a mountain of stone. So you dig an oval trench and pack it with concrete, a ring over twelve meters thick. This is the foundation. Now you haul in travertine, a hard, pale limestone, and stack it into eighty piers, pinned together with iron clamps. But solid walls would swallow too much stone. So you span from pier to pier with arches. This is an arcade. You stack three rings of arches, then a plain top wall. But the crowd needs seats. So you pour sloping concrete vaults between the piers and lay rows of seats on top. But the Roman sun is brutal. So you fix two hundred and forty wooden masts around the rim and stretch a giant awning from them. This is the velarium. It opened in the year eighty. Congratulations. You have built the Colosseum.
