# Sources: "You built a water mill" (2026-11-11)

| Claim in the script or caption | Source |
|---|---|
| Overshot wheel: buckets on the rim; water enters at the top and its fall turns the wheel | Penn State, *Vertical waterwheel*: overshot wheel "is a series of buckets attached to the outside of a big circle … The water goes into a container at the top and drops all the way down." https://engr.psu.edu/MTAH/articles/vertical_waterwheel.htm |
| Channels/troughs lead water to the wheel (mill race, flume, chute) | Same page: "Canals or mill races are needed to lead water from reservoirs or streams to the waterwheel"; "Sluice gates, flumes, and chutes are often used to control and direct the flow of water onto the wheel." Also Goodspeed Histories: water directed "through a headrace and/or a penstock — a wooden trough". https://goodspeedhistories.com/overshot-v-undershot/ |
| Overshot about 65% efficient vs undershot about 25% ("over twice the old kind"; measured by engineer John Smeaton) | Penn State page: Smeaton found "The average overshot wheel was far more efficient than the undershot, about 65% as opposed to 25%." (same URL as above) |
| Gearing: a toothed drum on the wheel's axle drives a smaller horizontal toothed drum attached to the millstone; a hopper feeds the grain | Vitruvius, *On Architecture* 10.5 (Morgan tr.): "a drum with teeth is fixed into one end of the axle"; a smaller toothed drum "set horizontally" is attached to the millstone; "the teeth of the drum which is fixed to the axle make the teeth of the horizontal drum move, and cause the mill to turn"; a hopper above supplies the corn. https://lexundria.com/vitr/10.5/mg |
| By 1086 one English survey (Domesday Book) counted over 6,000 mills | Mills Archive / Windmill World, *Mills in the Domesday Book*: the survey "listed the number of mills, with a total of over 6000 mentioned" (table: 6,026 mills in 4,185 places; figures approximate). https://windmillworld.com/watermills/domesday.htm |
| Vertical waterwheel dates to a century or two BC (background only; not used as a number in script) | Penn State page, same URL: "Invented a century or two before the time of Christ." |

Softened or general wording (no number): hand-grinding taking hours, "barely one loaf" (narrative framing, not a factual claim).
