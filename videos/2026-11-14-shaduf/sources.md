# Sources: "You built a shaduf" (2026-11-14)

| Claim in the script or caption | Source |
|---|---|
| A shaduf is a long pole on an upright frame (one post or a pair), pivoted about one-fifth of its length from one end; a bucket hangs from the long end and a counterweight sits on the short end | Wikipedia, *Shadoof*: a pole on a pivot "about one-fifth of its length from one end"; the frame "can be one pole or a pair". https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shadoof |
| The counterweight was often a large stone or a ball of dried mud (clay) | Akvopedia, *Counterpoise lift*: counterweight "often a large stone or a ball of dried mud". https://akvopedia.org/wiki/Counterpoise_lift ; Wikipedia: counterweight "of clay, stone, or similar material". |
| The operator pulls the empty bucket down into the water; the counterweight lifts it full; the bucket is emptied by swinging/tipping it out | Akvopedia: the bucket hangs on a rope from the long arm and is emptied by a sideways tipping motion. Wikipedia (same URL as above). |
| Water is tipped into irrigation channels that feed the fields | Wikipedia, *Shadoof*: used to lift water for irrigation into channels/fields; ScienceInsights, *What Is a Shaduf and How Does It Work?*: water is emptied into an irrigation channel for a plot of vegetables or grain. https://scienceinsights.org/what-is-a-shaduf-and-how-does-it-work/ |
| Buckets could be a skin bag or a reed basket coated in bitumen (natural tar) | Wikipedia, *Shadoof*: "a bucket, skin bag, or bitumen-coated reed basket". |
| About 2,000 litres an hour lifted by one person | Akvopedia: "about 2000 litres" lifted from 2-3 m depth in one hour. (Wikipedia, Chad field study: 39-130 L/min per man.) |
| Over 4,000 years old, in Mesopotamia | Wikipedia: earliest evidence is a cylinder seal showing a shadoof, dated to about 2200 BCE, Mesopotamia (time of Sargon of Akkad). |
| Used in Egypt (caption hashtag #ancientegypt) | Wikipedia: appeared in Upper Egypt sometime after 2000 BC. |
| Some farmers still use it today | Wikipedia: still used in many areas of Africa and Asia; Akvopedia: common in Egypt, Nigeria, Niger and Chad. |

Softened or narrative wording (no number): "the bucket leaks half its water", "watered three rows by noon", and the bank height are story framing, not historical claims.
