# You built a canal lock

Hook arm B (question).

How did a boat climb a hill without leaving the water?
Your barges carry grain along a canal.
But the land rises, and water won't run uphill.
So you build a ramp, and haul the boats up and over.
But hulls crack, and grain spills into the mud.
So you build two gates across the canal, more than fifty paces apart.
Between them sits a stone chamber.
This is a lock.
A boat floats in, and you shut the gate behind it.
Then you lift wooden boards in the top gate.
Water pours in, and the boat rises with it.
When the levels match, you open the top gate and sail on.
To go down, you let the water out.
But a gate that lifts straight up is heavy, and slow.
So you hang two wooden doors that meet in a V, pointing upstream.
The water pushes them shut, tighter and tighter.
This is a mitre gate.
Leonardo da Vinci may have drawn one for Milan.
Then you add paddles, small hatches you crank open.
A Chinese engineer built the first lock like this over a thousand years ago.
Gates like these still work today.
Congratulations. You have built a canal lock.
