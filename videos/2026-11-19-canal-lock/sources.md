# Sources: canal lock

- Pound lock pioneered by Song engineer Qiao Weiyue in 984, replacing double slipways where soldiers plotted with bandits to wreck heavy grain barges and steal the grain. The Song Shi says the two gates were "rather more than 50 paces" apart, with a level change of 4 or 5 feet (1.2 or 1.5 m) at each lock. Filled by raising wooden baulks in the top gate; emptied by lowering them and raising ones in the lower gate. A lock operation takes 10 to 20 minutes. Paddles are flat panels lifted by a rack and pinion. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lock_(water_navigation)
- Mitre gates "invented by Leonardo da Vinci sometime around the late 15th century" (Wikipedia, same page). Britannica: the mitred gate "may have been invented by Leonardo da Vinci for the San Marco Lock in Milan"; the leaves are angled into the force of the stream. https://www.britannica.com/technology/mitre-gate
- "The double mitre lock uses the force of the water to keep them tightly shut"; paddles are wound open and water fills or empties the lock through sluices. First lock with gates at both ends in Europe: Holland, 1373. https://canalrivertrust.org.uk/canals-and-rivers/how-does-a-canal-lock-work
- Leonardo's miter lock is documented in a notebook from 1497; the design is still used today (Panama and Suez named in the headline). https://www.openculture.com/2023/11/leonardo-da-vinci-created-the-design-for-the-miter-lock-which-is-still-used-in-the-panama-and-suez-canals.html
