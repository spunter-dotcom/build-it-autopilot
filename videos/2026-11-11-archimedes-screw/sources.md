# Sources: "You built an Archimedes screw" (2026-11-11)

| Claim in the script or caption | Source |
|---|---|
| Built from a rounded wooden beam whose ends are divided into eight parts | Vitruvius, *De Architectura* X.6.1: "A beam is procured … this is rounded." The ends are divided "into four or eight parts" by diameters. https://penelope.uchicago.edu/Thayer/E/Roman/Texts/Vitruvius/10*.html |
| Thin willow strips smeared with pitch are wound around it in a spiral, building up into eight channels | Vitruvius X.6.2: "a small flexible ruler of willow or withy, smeared with liquid pitch" is run around the shaft, making "eight channels round the shaft, in the form of a screw." (same URL) |
| Wrapped in planks, sealed with pitch, bound with iron hoops | Vitruvius X.6.3: "planks are fastened all round, saturated with pitch, and bound with iron hoops, that the water may not injure them." (same URL) |
| Iron pins at each end resting on a frame | Vitruvius X.6.3: shaft ends get "iron nails and hoops" and iron pivots that sit in a frame of beams. (same URL) |
| Tilt: 3 feet up for every 5 feet along (about 37°) | Vitruvius X.6.4: the inclination "is equal to the right angled triangle of Pythagoras: that is, if the length be divided into five parts, three of these will give the height". arcsin(3/5) ≈ 36.9° (calculation). (same URL) |
| The low end scoops up water; pockets are pushed up the tube as it turns | Wikipedia, *Archimedes' screw*: "As the shaft turns, the bottom end scoops up a volume of water … pushed up the tube by the rotating helicoid." https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Archimedes%27_screw |
| Water that leaks back is carried up by the next segment | Wikipedia (same page): "If water from one section leaks into the next lower one, it will be transferred upwards by the next segment." |
| Used for over 2,000 years; earliest written mention c. 250–220 BC, still used today | Wikipedia (same page): earliest mention by Moschion "between 250 and 220 BC"; modern uses include drainage, sewage plants and screw turbines |
| Named after Archimedes of Syracuse (traditionally credited) | Wikipedia (same page): "Archimedes of Syracuse is traditionally credited with inventing the water screw" (some scholars think he found it already in use in Egypt) |
| Used for irrigation (Nile), draining mines and ships' bilges | Wikipedia (same page): Diodorus on Nile irrigation; Posidonius on Spanish mines; Moschion on Hiero II's ship |
| Roman engineer Vitruvius wrote the recipe down about 2,000 years ago | Wikipedia (same page) dates *De Architectura* to "27-22 BC" |

Notes
- The crank is this video's design choice (the owner asked for one). Vitruvius describes the screw being turned "by the treading of men", so the script never says the Romans used a crank.
- The video's "eight twisting blades" follows Vitruvius's eight channels.
