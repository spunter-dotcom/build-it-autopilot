# Sources: Log cabin

- Scandinavians brought the log cabin to America when they settled New Sweden, around Delaware Bay, in 1638. https://philadelphiaencyclopedia.org/archive/log-cabins
- About 600 Swedish and Finnish emigrants settled New Sweden in the following years and built the first log cabins in America. https://gscw.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/01/1638-New-Sweden.pdf
- The Nothnagle log cabin in Gibbstown, NJ may date to as early as 1638 and is considered the oldest surviving log cabin in the US. https://chasingfamilyghosts.com/post/new-sweden-the-time-before-penn
- The saddle notch is a rounded groove cut to cradle the log below; logs settle tightly as they shrink. Gaps were sealed with chinking: moss, clay, straw, later lime mixes. https://www.designingbuildings.co.uk/wiki/Log_construction
- Rigid fillers (wood, stone) plus soft fillers (moss, clay), then daubing of clay, mud or lime. https://ophelia.sdsu.edu:8443/henryford_org/03-25-2015/research/caring/log.aspx.html
- Cabin corners were often set on large stones along the sill (bottom log). https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chinking
