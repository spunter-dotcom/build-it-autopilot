Winter is coming, and your family needs a home. But all you have is a forest and an axe. So you fell tall, straight trees and trim them into logs. But a log lying on wet dirt will rot. So you set a row of flat stones and lay your first log right on top. This is a sill log. Now the corners. You carve a curved notch into each log so the one above drops in snug and locks in place. This is a saddle notch. Log by log four walls rise, locked together at every corner, but the wind whistles through every gap. So you stuff the gaps with wood chips, moss and clay, then smear clay and lime over the top. This is chinking. You saw out a door and a window and raise a sloped roof to shed the rain, but the nights are freezing. So you stack stones into a chimney and light a fire. Settlers in New Sweden brought this design to America in 1638. Congratulations! You have built a log cabin.
