# You built a lime kiln

How did a village hold its stone walls together, long before cement?
Your new barn is stacked stones, and every storm knocks a piece down. You need mortar.
But mud washes out in the rain.
So you look at the rock under your fields. Limestone.
You break it into small lumps and throw out the dust, so the fire can breathe.
You dig into a hillside and build a deep stone pot, with an arch at the bottom. This is a lime kiln.
So you fill it in layers. Wood, then limestone, then wood again, all the way to the top.
You light it at the bottom, and the fire climbs up through the stone.
But it has to get hotter than nine hundred degrees, and stay there for three days. So you feed it, day and night.
Then you let it cool, and rake the stone out through the arch. It's white now, and lighter. This is quicklime.
Pour on water, and it hisses, steams and turns into a soft white putty.
So you mix it with sand and spread it between your stones. Slowly, it turns back into stone.
Paint the rest on your walls, and spread some on your fields.
Congratulations. You have built a lime kiln.
