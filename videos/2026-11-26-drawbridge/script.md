# You built a drawbridge

How do you keep an army out of your castle, but still let your own carts in?
So you dig a deep ditch around the stone walls, and you let it fill with water. This is a moat.
But now your own people can't get in either.
So you lay a thick wooden bridge across the water, hinged at the gate.
But a bridge that never moves lets the enemy march straight in.
So you fix chains to the far end, and run them up into a room above the gate.
But the bridge is far too heavy to haul up by hand.
So you wind the chains around a drum and turn it with handles. This is a windlass.
But it's still slow, and your guards are worn out.
So you hang heavy weights on the chains, and the bridge almost lifts itself. By the fourteenth century, builders added long lifting arms above the bridge, with counterweights on their inner ends.
Now, when enemies appear, a few guards turn the handles, and the bridge swings up flat against the gate. Behind it, a heavy grate drops down. This is a portcullis.
Congratulations. You have built a drawbridge.
