# Viking longship

Your people need a ship that can cross the open sea and still run up onto a beach.
So you start with one long oak timber, laid on the sand. This is a keel.
Then you raise a curved post at each end, the bow and the stern.
But you have no saw. So you split oak logs and shape every plank with an axe.
So you lay the first plank along the keel, and the next one overlaps it, like roof tiles.
You fix each overlap with iron rivets, about every fifteen centimetres. This is clinker building.
Sixteen planks a side, and the hull is just a thin shell.
But a shell can twist in waves. So you fit ribs inside, after the planks are on.
But how do you steer? So you hang one big rudder on the right side, the steering board. This is the starboard side.
But the wind will not always help. So you cut holes for thirty-two oars.
And when it does help, you raise a square sail of about one hundred and ten square metres.
Some longships needed only about three feet of water. So you row up rivers and land right on the beach.
Congratulations. You have built a Viking longship.
