Florence wants a giant for its cathedral. All it has is one tall block of marble, cut from the mountains at Carrara and left lying in a yard for twenty-five years. But the stone is thin, and full of tiny holes and veins. Two sculptors before you have left it unfinished. So you take the job anyway. You are twenty-six. You pick up a mallet and a heavy iron spike. This is a point chisel. So you knock off big chunks, until a rough figure stands inside the block. But the point leaves deep pits. So you switch to a blade with teeth. This is a claw chisel. You comb the stone in close lines, and the shape tightens. But the block is thin, so there is no stone to spare. So you keep the figure slender, front to back. Then you file it smooth with a rough steel bar. This is a rasp. And you polish it with fine grit. But the giant stands seventeen feet tall, half a mile from its square. So you hang it in a wooden frame, and forty men roll it over greased logs. It takes four days. Congratulations. You have sculpted David.
