# You built a stone arch bridge

Hook arm B (question).

How did people cross a river before steel?
Your town needs the market on the far bank.
But wooden bridges rot, and they burn.
So you build in stone.
But you can't lay stone underwater.
So you drive a ring of posts into the riverbed and seal it with clay.
This is a cofferdam.
You empty it, and build a stone pier on the dry bottom.
You give it a pointed nose, so the current splits around it.
You even leave a hole in the pier, so flood water can pass through.
But stone can't hang in the air.
So you build a wooden frame shaped like a half circle.
This is centering.
Then you lay wedge-shaped stones up both sides of the frame.
The last one drops into the top and locks the arch.
This is a keystone.
Now you knock the frame away, and the arch holds itself, every stone pressing on the next.
So you add a second arch, fill the sides, and pave the top.
More than two thousand years ago, Rome built one like this over the Tiber.
People still walk across it today.
Congratulations. You have built a stone arch bridge.
