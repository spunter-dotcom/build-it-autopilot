# Sources: ice house (2026-11-21)

- Ice came from a nearby lake or pool, harvested in the depths of winter. Country Life, "The ice house uncovered" (2010): https://www.countrylife.co.uk/comment-opinion/the-ice-house-uncovered-20789
- Workers cut blocks of ice, pulled them to shore and carted them to the ice house; water was poured on the ice to make "a solid mass". Mount Vernon: https://mountvernon.org/the-estate-gardens/ice
- Washington's ice house was a dry well dug into the hillside; its top was arched so dirt and sod could add insulation; a layer of straw lined it. Mount Vernon (same URL).
- Below-ground icehouses at country houses from about 1600, usually brick-lined, "typically with the profile of a dome and cup"; ice "packed between straw layers"; by the later 18th century "virtually every country house had one"; Deepdene's corridor had "two sets of insulating doors". Historic England list entry 1448836: https://historicengland.org.uk/listing/the-list/list-entry/1448836
- A drain hole in the base let meltwater drain away; a full ice house could take up to 18 months to thaw; James I commissioned the first modern ice house in Greenwich Park in 1619; Hampton Court's (1625-6) was a brick-lined well 30 ft (9.1 m) deep and 16 ft wide. Country Life (same URL).
- General background: Pennsylvania field guide, ice house: https://www.phmc.state.pa.us/portal/communities/agriculture/field-guide/ice-house.html
