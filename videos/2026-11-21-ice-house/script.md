# You built an ice house

How do you keep ice frozen all summer, with no electricity?
It is winter, and the lake has frozen thick. So you cut it into blocks and cart them up the bank.
But in a shed, the summer sun melts them away. So you dig a deep pit into the hillside, where the earth stays cool.
But bare soil crumbles. So you line the pit with brick, shaped like a cup, up to nine metres deep.
This is an ice well.
But meltwater pools at the bottom and eats the ice. So you leave a drain hole in the floor.
But an open top lets in sun and rain. So you close it with a brick dome, and heap earth and turf on top.
This is the dome and cup.
Now you pack the blocks in, with straw between every layer, and splash on water so it freezes into one solid mass.
But every time you open the door, warm air rushes in. So you build a narrow passage with two sets of doors.
Kings built these from sixteen nineteen. By the late seventeen hundreds, nearly every country house had one, and a full one could keep ice for up to eighteen months.
Congratulations. You have built an ice house.
