# Ballista: sources

- Heron of Alexandria places the invention of the gastraphetes ("belly bow") "some unknown time before c. 420 BC"; the long-dominant view (Marsden) credits Dionysius I of Syracuse in 399 BC, now disputed. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gastraphetes
- The gastraphetes outranged the hand-drawn composite bow by "between 50 and 100 meters". https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gastraphetes
- "In 399 in Syracuse, King Dionysius I ... assembled a large group of engineers." https://warhistory.org/de/article/torsion-catapults?amp=1
- "The first torsion-spring catapults were made by Macedonian engineers between 353 and 341 BCE" (stated as "it is believed"). Same URL.
- A ballista has two non-flexible arms set in "springs" made from sinew, with a heavy sinew string, slider, winch, ratchet apparatus and trigger mechanism. Same URL.
- Stone-throwers fired stones "weighing from 13 to 26 kilograms, although stones as large as 162 kilograms are known to have been fired." Same URL.
- Stone-throwing artillery "needed to be within 150 meters of a fortification to be effective." Same URL.
- The Romans called the torsion catapult a ballista. Same URL.

Not sourced, so left out: Roman counts per legion, exact ranges of the ballista itself, iron-frame versions.
