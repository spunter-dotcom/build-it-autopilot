# You built a ballista

How do you break a city wall without dying at its foot?
Your king in Syracuse wants a weapon that hits harder than any archer.
So you build a bigger bow. Too stiff to draw by arm, so you brace it against your belly and push.
This is a gastraphetes. A belly bow.
But wood can only bend so far.
So you stop bending. You start twisting.
You wind thick bundles of animal sinew around a strong wooden frame and crank them tight.
This is a torsion spring.
Two stiff wooden arms slot into the twisted springs, joined by a heavy sinew string.
But nobody can pull that string by hand.
So you add a winch at the back.
And a ratchet, so every click holds the string where it is.
Then a slider down the middle to carry the shot, and a trigger to let it go.
This is a ballista.
So you load a stone. Thirteen to twenty-six kilograms is normal. Some crews threw one hundred sixty-two.
But stones need to land within one hundred fifty meters of the wall.
So you move closer, wind, aim, and pull the trigger.
The arms slam forward. The stone flies. The wall cracks.
Congratulations. You have built a ballista.
