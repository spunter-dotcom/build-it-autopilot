# You built a trireme

Hook arm A (statement; the previous video, the stone arch bridge, was B).

Your city needs a fleet to stop an invasion.
But you have no cannons and no engines, just timber, rope and people.
So you build a ship that is all speed.
You lay a long oak keel, about thirty-seven meters long.
Then you fit the planks edge to edge, and lock them with hidden wooden pegs.
But a hull that long and thin bends in the waves.
So you stretch thick ropes inside it, from bow to stern, and twist them tight like a bowstring.
Now it needs power.
But one row of oars is not enough.
So you stack three rows of rowers, one above the other.
Sixty-two on top, fifty-four in the middle, fifty-four at the bottom.
One hundred seventy people, pulling together.
This is a trireme.
But how does a wooden ship sink another one?
So you cast a heavy bronze beak and fit it to the bow, low at the waterline.
This is a ram.
Now you row straight at the enemy and punch a hole in their hull.
About twenty-five hundred years ago, at Salamis, ships like this beat the Persian fleet.
And in nineteen eighty-seven, a modern copy hit nine knots on oar power alone.
Congratulations. You have built a trireme.
