# Sources: Greek trireme

- [Olympias (trireme), Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Olympias_(trireme))
  - Reconstruction length 36.9 m, beam 5.5 m, 47 tonnes; built 1985–1987 in Piraeus, launched August 1987.
  - 170 oarsmen: 62 thranitai (upper), 54 zygitai (middle), 54 thalamitai (lower); full classical crew about 200.
  - Achieved 9 knots (17 km/h) in a 1987 exercise with 170 volunteer rowers.
  - Hulls of planks joined with closely spaced, pegged mortise-and-tenon joints.
  - Hypozomata: tightened ropes beneath the deck from bow to stern, pre-tensioning the hull.
  - Bronze bow ram of 200 kg (copy of an original in the Piraeus museum).
- [Trireme, World History Encyclopedia (Mark Cartwright)](https://www.worldhistory.org/trireme/)
  - Length around 37 m; outer hull of oak; planks closely fitting edge to edge (not overlapping), sealed with pitch; hypozomata ropes for strength.
  - Three levels of rowers, 170–180 in all; bronze-sheathed ram on the prow.
  - Battle of Salamis, 480 BCE, led by Themistocles.
- [Triremes, History Skills](https://www.historyskills.com/classroom/ancient-history/triremes/)
  - Average length 37 m; 170 oarsmen in three tiers; bronze ram (embolon) punched the enemy hull below the waterline; Salamis 480 BCE.
- [Battle of Salamis, Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/event/Battle-of-Salamis) and [historyofwar.org](https://www.historyofwar.org/articles/battles_salamis_480.html)
  - The Greek fleet defeated the Persian fleet at Salamis in 480 BC (about 2,500 years ago).

Softened or cut: "keel of oak" follows WHE (Olympias itself used iroko); "twist them tight" describes tensioning in plain words; ramming speed estimates (16 knots) cut as unsourced.
