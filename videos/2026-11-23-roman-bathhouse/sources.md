# Sources: Roman bathhouse with hypocaust

- Hypocaust raises the floor on pilae stacks; hot air circulates under it; layers: a layer of tiles, then concrete, then the floor tiles; hot air and smoke rise through clay or tile flues in the walls to outlets in the roof; warmest rooms placed nearest the furnace, heat regulated by the amount of wood; Vitruvius credits Sergius Orata, c. 80 BC (disputed by Fagan 1996); Temple of Ephesus heated this way in 350 BC; Vitruvius placed caldaria next to the tepidarium to save fuel. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hypocaust
- Pilae stacks are stacks of square or round tiles; air rises through hollow bricks into the walls. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pilae_stacks
- Praefurnium: the wood-fired furnace room, lower than the heated rooms; fuel wood or charcoal; water heated in copper or bronze tanks above the furnace. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Praefurnium
- Floors paved with 60 cm square tiles (bipedales, "two-footers"); hollow rectangular wall tubes (tubuli); frigidarium = cold room, unheated; tepidarium = warm room; calidarium = hot room with hot-water pool; Baths of Caracalla (c. 235 CE) heated by 50 furnaces burning ten tons of wood a day. https://www.worldhistory.org/Roman_Baths/
- Caldarium was the hottest room; hot air rose through hollowed-out bricks in the walls and out through chimneys; water and air may have risen well above 100 °F. https://www.pbs.org/wgbh/nova/lostempires/roman/caldarium.html
