# You built a Roman bathhouse

How did the Romans keep a bath hot all winter, with no electricity?
Your town wants a place to bathe. So you build a long brick hall, with a cold pool.
This is a frigidarium.
But in winter, nobody wants to get in. So you light a fire on the floor. But the room fills with smoke.
So you build a furnace outside the wall, and feed it wood all day.
But how does the heat get in? So you lift the whole floor off the ground, on short stacks of tiles.
This is a hypocaust.
Now hot air flows under the floor, between the stacks. On top, you lay tiles, then concrete, then big square tiles, two feet across.
But the walls stay cold. So you line them with hollow clay tubes. Hot air rises through them and out at the roof.
But the room by the fire gets hottest. So you put the hot pool there, a warm room beside it, and the cold room furthest away.
This is a caldarium.
Over the furnace, you set a bronze tank, so the water heats too.
In the biggest baths in Rome, fifty furnaces burned ten tons of wood a day.
Congratulations. You have built a Roman bathhouse.
