# You built an obelisk

Your pharaoh wants a giant stone needle, cut from one single piece of granite.
So you go to the granite quarry at Aswan. But granite is far too hard to chop.
So you pound it. Workers slam balls of even harder stone into the rock, grinding a deep trench around the shape. Red marks on the wall track how far they get.
But granite can crack. One giant split, and it is still lying there, forty-two metres long and over a thousand tons.
This is the unfinished obelisk.
So you pick flawless stone, carve it free, and move it down to the river.
There, a huge barge carries it down the Nile to the temple. One record says these ships were sixty-three metres long.
But how do you stand it up? Nobody wrote it down. One idea: you build a ramp, with a pit of sand at its end.
You haul the obelisk up the ramp, base first, until it hangs over the pit.
Then you let the sand pour out, little by little. The obelisk sinks and tips, until its base drops into a groove on its pedestal.
So teams on ropes pull it upright.
This is an obelisk.
Congratulations. You have built an obelisk.
