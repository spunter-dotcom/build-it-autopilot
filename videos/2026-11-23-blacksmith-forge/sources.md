# Sources: Blacksmith forge with bellows

1. Kansas Historical Society, Kansapedia "Blacksmithing" (2012): https://www.kansashistory.gov/kansapedia/blacksmithing/17872
   - "A forge also must have a bellows or blower to control the admission of air to the burning fuel to regulate its heat."
   - Forges can be brick, metal or concrete, or wood "with earth packed inside of it for fireproofing."
   - The forge "is required to make metal hot enough to be shaped easily." "Blacksmiths forge metal by first heating and then hammering it."
   - "The most important single tool in the blacksmith shop is the anvil." Steel anvils are superior, "as only steel may be properly hardened."
   - Forge welding: two pieces heated "to a white heat in the forge until the surfaces are partially melted", then "fused together with a few correctly placed blows with the hammer."
   - Steel is hardened/tempered by heating it and "then quenching it in water or oil." Wrought iron cannot be, "because its carbon content is too low."
2. Penn State MTAH, "Techniques of the blacksmith" (from Kauffman, Metalworking Trades in Early America): https://engr.psu.edu/MTAH/articles/techniques_blacksmith.htm
   - Hearth kit: "the hearth with its bellows, water trough, shovels, tongs, rake, poker, and a water container for damping down the fire and cooling objects."
   - Charcoal fuel. "Snowball heat ... white heat; this is used to weld iron." Twisting at "a blood-red heat". Punching needs reheating, up to three times.
   - Tools: anvil, sledges, tongs, hammers, cross-pein hammer for drawing out.
3. Penn State MTAH, "Forge furnace" (Prof. Gerald Eggert): https://engr.psu.edu/mtah/essays/forge_furnace.htm
   - Forges "all used charcoal for fuel"; "anvils, tongs, and assorted other tools for manipulating hot bars of iron."
   - Cold pig iron "was no longer malleable"; a strong blast of air softened it.
