# Sources: olive oil press

- A Roman stone olive press known as a trapetum, from Pompeii: olives are poured into a large stone bowl (mortarium) and crushed under two concave stones (orbes) on a central beam, which turns on a central post set in the bowl. https://www.worldhistory.org/image/5645/trapetum-roman-olive-press/
- Traditional presses had a crushing basin with a millstone turned by people or a draft animal on a wooden beam; olives and pits were ground into pulp. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Olive_oil_extraction
- The pulp was put into "frails", flexible woven baskets, stacked one on top of the other; a stone weight released the oil. The weight was lifted by a lever beam resting on a pivot between two posts. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Olive_oil_extraction
- The liquid that runs off is oil and vegetable water (amurca, watery lees) mixed, into a pit; after settling, the oil rises to the surface and is removed by decantation. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Olive_oil_extraction
- Roman wine and oil presses were up to 12 metres long and 3-4 metres high, weighing many tonnes, built by rural people from local stone and wood. https://pursuit.unimelb.edu.au/articles/a-pressing-matter-ancient-roman-food-technology
- Cato (2nd century BCE) describes a press worked with a large winch and ropes; reconstructed traditional press at the Eretz Israel Museum uses a lever (6 m long) and wooden screw (4 m high) to raise the limestone weight, with baskets of fruit under the lever. Roman screws were hand-carved from wood; a 3rd-century sarcophagus shows men turning a screw to raise a weight-stone. https://pursuit.unimelb.edu.au/articles/a-pressing-matter-ancient-roman-food-technology
- Traditional lever-and-screw presses were still used up to the mid-20th century (a 1950s Lebanese village). https://pursuit.unimelb.edu.au/articles/a-pressing-matter-ancient-roman-food-technology
- Heron of Alexandria is credited with a twin-screw oil press (two screws push a plank down on the pulp). https://kotsanas.com/exhibits/agricultural/the-twin-screw-oil-press/
