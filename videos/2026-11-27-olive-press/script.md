# You built an olive press

Your village is full of olive trees, and you need oil. For lamps, for cooking, for trade.
But squeezing olives by hand gives you a mess and a few drops.
So you carve a round stone bowl, set a post in the middle, and hang two heavy stone wheels on a beam across it.
You push the beam around, and the wheels crush the olives, pits and all, into paste. This is a trapetum.
But paste isn't oil. So you scoop it into flat woven baskets and stack them up.
Now you need weight. A lot of it.
So you fix a long timber beam into the wall, and lay it across the pile.
Then you tie a huge stone to the free end. The beam sinks, and oil starts to run. This is a lever press.
But lifting that stone every time wears you out.
So you carve a giant wooden screw through the end of the beam, and turn it. Now the stone climbs on its own.
The juice runs into a pit, oil and bitter water mixed.
So you wait. The oil rises to the top, and you pour it off. The biggest presses were up to twelve metres long.
Congratulations. You have built an olive press.
