# You built a Roman road

Your legions need to march across the empire, fast, in any weather.
So you need a road. But dirt turns to mud the moment it rains.
So your surveyors mark out a dead-straight line.
Then you dig a long trench along it.
You fill it with rough gravel and crushed brick, pounded hard.
This is the rudus. The foundation.
But loose stone spreads out under heavy carts.
So you set upright curb stones along both edges to lock it all in.
Then you spread a layer of finer gravel on top.
This is the nucleus. The core.
But water is still your enemy.
So you shape the surface higher in the middle, so rain runs off into the drains.
Then you lay big fitted slabs of hard stone across the top.
You make it about four meters wide, so two carts can pass.
But carts keep rolling onto the walkers' path.
So every few meters, you set a taller block into the curb.
And every thousand paces, you plant a stone that tells travelers how far they've come.
This is a milestone. Each one names who keeps that stretch repaired.
In three hundred twelve BC, the Appian Way headed south from Rome.
Congratulations. You have built a Roman road.
