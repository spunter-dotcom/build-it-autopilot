# Sources: Roman road

- https://www.worldhistory.org/article/758/roman-roads/ (Mark Cartwright, World History Encyclopedia)
  - "Major roads were around a standard 4.2 m wide, which was enough space for two wheeled vehicles to pass each other."
  - "First, a trench was dug and a foundation (rudus) was laid using rough gravel, crushed brick, clay materials or even wooden piles in marshy areas, and set between curb stones."
  - "On top of this a layer of finer gravel was added (nucleus) and the road was then surfaced with blocks or slabs (summum dorsum)."
  - "Roads were purposely inclined slightly from the centre down to the curb to allow rainwater to run off along the sides, and for the same purpose many also had drains and drainage canals."
  - Footpath 1-3 m wide each side behind the curb; "every 3-5 metres there was a higher block set into the curb" to keep wheeled traffic off the footpath.
  - Surveying was done "to ensure the proposed route was actually straight."
  - Via Appia "Constructed from 312 BCE and covering 196 km (132 Roman miles), it linked Rome to Capua"; named after the censor Appius Claudius Caecus; later 569 km to Brundisium.
  - "The network of public Roman roads covered over 120,000 km."
  - Milestones "set up at regular intervals" recording who maintained the stretch.
- https://www.scientificamerican.com/article/new-high-resolution-map-transforms-what-we-know-about-roman-roads-and-the-roman-empire/
  - Via Appia "constructed starting in 312 B.C.E.", "the oldest and best-known road of the Roman Empire."
  - Roman mile = mille passus, 1,000 paces, about 1.48 km. Milestones "informed travelers of their location and distance."
  - New map: about 300,000 km of roads in the 2nd century CE (8,000 km located with certainty).
