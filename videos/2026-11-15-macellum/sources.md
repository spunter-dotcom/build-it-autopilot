# Sources: Roman macellum (food market)

- A macellum was "an ancient Roman indoor market building that sold mostly provisions (especially meat and fish)"; "a food market, particularly for meat, fish and delicatessen". https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Macellum
- Layout: "shops arranged around a courtyard which contains a central tholos"; the building is usually square; the shops (tabernae) were "all of the same size". https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Macellum
- The tholos is "a round structure, usually built upon a couple of steps (a podium), with a ring of columns supporting a domed roof"; it was "well provided with water and drains", and some markets had a fountain or water feature there. It is thought to be where fish was sold, because fish skeletons were excavated. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Macellum
- Butchers' shops "were provided with marble counters, presumably to keep the meat cooler, and drains for the removal of water and fluid waste". https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Macellum
- Pompeii: twelve column bases mark the central round space; "many fish bones and scales" were found in its drainage trough, and fish were gutted and cleaned there. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Macellum_of_Pompeii
- Pompeii: the tholos "was also used for selling and cleaning fish"; the building "was built between 130-120 BC". https://pompeiisites.org/en/archaeological-site/macellum/
- Pompeii: the earlier open marketplace had "a carefully polished and neatly compacted stone pavement"; the interior portico had a water gully. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Macellum_of_Pompeii
- Rome: the Macellum Magnum was "built and dedicated by the emperor Nero in AD 59". https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Macellum

Softened or generic wording (no specific source claimed): "no ice", "spoils fast", clay roof tiles, "sellers need shade". The script describes a typical macellum, not one exact building; at Pompeii the central roof stood on wooden poles.
