# Sources: noria

- A noria is an undershot water wheel driven by the river; its rim carries containers or compartments that lift water from the river to an aqueduct at the top of the wheel. — https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Noria
- Hellenistic engineers used compartmented wheels in the 3rd–2nd century BC; Vitruvius described the noria in the 1st century BC; Romans around 300 AD replaced wooden compartments with ceramic pots. — https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Noria
- Name from Arabic nā‘ūra, "to groan" / "grunt". — https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Noria
- Hama, Syria: a series of 17 norias on the Orontes. The river pushes the collection boxes under water, where they fill, then they are carried to the top and empty into an aqueduct built on tall stone arches. — https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Norias_of_Hama
- Axles of walnut, most other parts poplar; the wooden axles make powerful groaning sounds. — https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Norias_of_Hama
- Largest wheel, Noria al-Muhammadiyya: 21 m (69 ft) diameter, 120 water collection boxes (4–12 litres each), raises 200,000 litres per hour, one revolution per minute; inscription year 763 AH = 1361 CE. — https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Norias_of_Hama
- Noria al-Muhammadiyya built 763 AH / 1361 CE, 21 m (69 ft); fourteen arches of its aqueduct survive; restored 1977; ASME landmark #241. — https://www.asme.org/about-asme/engineering-history/landmarks/241-noria-al-muhammadiyya
