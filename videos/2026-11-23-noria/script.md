# You built a noria

Your fields sit high above the river, and they are dying of thirst.
So you carry water up the bank in jars. But it takes all day, and the crops still wilt.
So you build a giant wooden wheel, and stand it in the river. The current pushes the paddles, and the wheel turns by itself.
But turning is not lifting. So you fix wooden boxes around the rim. Each box dips under the water, fills, and rides up to the top.
This is a noria.
But at the top, the water just spills back into the river. So you build a stone channel on tall arches, right beside the wheel, and the boxes tip into it.
This is an aqueduct.
But the wheel is too small to reach your highest fields. So you make it bigger. Much bigger. Twenty-one metres across, with one hundred and twenty boxes on its rim.
But the thick wooden axle groans as it turns. You keep it anyway. The name noria comes from an Arabic word for groaning.
In Hama, in Syria, the biggest wheel was built in thirteen sixty-one. It could lift two hundred thousand litres of water an hour.
Congratulations. You have built a noria.
