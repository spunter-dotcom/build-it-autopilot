# Sources: Inca grass-rope suspension bridge (Q'eswachaka)

- Inca rope bridges were built from ichu grass; each cable was replaced every year as a mit'a (labour) obligation; reconstructed every June. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Inca_rope_bridge
- Q'eswachaka: Ichu grass; total length 28 metres (92 ft), width 1.2 m; over the Apurímac River near Huinchiri, Quehue District, Canas Province, Peru; renewed annually in the second week of June; trimmed mat rolls form the deck. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Queshuachaca
- UNESCO: families cut and twist straw "to form thin ropes about 70 m long", twist them "to form medium-sized ropes, which are then braided to form six great ropes"; men "tie the ropes tightly to ancient stone bases"; "the renewal lasts only three days"; communities of Huinchiri, Chaupibanda, Choccayhua and Ccollana Quehue; inscribed on the Representative List in 2013. https://ich.unesco.org/en/RL/knowledge-skills-and-rituals-related-to-the-annual-renewal-of-the-qeswachaka-bridge-00594
- Smithsonian (NMAI): small cords twisted from harvested grass, twisted into larger ropes, braided into main cables; "stone abutments anchor the cables on either side"; the cables "form the floor and handrails"; "Sticks woven across the floor every few feet help keep the bridge from twisting"; floor covered with matting; "it takes only three days"; "500-year-old tradition"; builders "cut [the old bridge] down and let it fall into the river"; Inka road network "nearly 25,000 miles". https://www.smithsonianmag.com/blogs/national-museum-american-indian/2017/08/05/qeswachaka-last-inka-suspension-bridge/
