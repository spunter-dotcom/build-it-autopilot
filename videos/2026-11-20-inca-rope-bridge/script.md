# You built an Inca rope bridge

Hook arm A (statement).

Your village needs to cross a deep river gorge.
But all you have is stone and grass.
So you build two stone bases, one on each cliff.
But stone alone can't span the gap.
So you cut the tough mountain grass, called ichu.
You twist it into thin cords, about seventy metres long.
But one cord snaps like a thread.
So you twist the thin cords into thicker ropes.
Then you braid those ropes into huge cables.
This is a main cable.
You make six of them.
Your neighbours haul them across the gorge and tie them tight to the stone bases.
Some cables become the floor. The others become handrails.
But the floor twists and sways in the wind.
So you weave sticks across the floor every few feet.
Then you cover it with grass mats.
This is a suspension bridge.
It stretches twenty-eight metres over the Apurímac River.
But grass doesn't last forever.
So every June, four communities rebuild it in just three days.
Then they cut the old bridge loose and let it fall into the river.
People have kept this up for five hundred years.
Congratulations. You have built an Inca rope bridge.
