# You built a lighthouse

How did ships find a harbour at night, before electric light?
You rule Alexandria, a port in Egypt. But the coast is flat, and hidden shoals wreck ships in the dark.
So you light a fire on the beach. But far out at sea, a beach fire is too low to see.
So you raise it. Offshore sits the island of Pharos. You join it to the city with a stone causeway more than a kilometre long.
This is the Heptastadion.
There, you stack limestone and granite blocks into a square tower, thirty metres wide.
But you need more height. So you add a narrower eight-sided tier, then a round one on top.
But fuel has to get up there. So you build a ramp inside, wide enough, one visitor wrote, for two horsemen side by side.
At the top, you build a furnace. At night, a fire burns, and later writers say a bronze mirror threw its light out to sea. On the roof, a statue of Zeus.
This is the Pharos.
It rises over one hundred metres, one of the tallest buildings on Earth for centuries. Even today, the word for lighthouse in French, Italian and Spanish comes from its name.
Congratulations. You have built a lighthouse.
