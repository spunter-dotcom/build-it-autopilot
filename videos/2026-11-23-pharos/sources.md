# Sources: Lighthouse of Alexandria (Pharos)

- Built on the island of Pharos, a small island on the western edge of the Nile Delta, during the reign of Ptolemy II (280–247 BC); commissioned by Ptolemy I; dated "between 284 and 246 BC" (so about 2,300 years ago). Took twelve years; cost 800 talents of silver. — https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pharos
- Ptolemy I commissioned it around 300 BCE; completed some 20 years later; Pliny: "The cost of its erection was eight hundred talents"; Pliny: its fires warn ships of the neighbouring shoals. — https://www.worldhistory.org/Lighthouse_of_Alexandria/
- Heptastadion causeway linking island and city: more than 1,200 m / about 1.2 km. — https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pharos ; https://www.worldhistory.org/Lighthouse_of_Alexandria/
- Built of solid blocks of limestone and granite; three sections: square lower section, octagonal middle, circular top; base 30 m per side (Balawi, 1166). — https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pharos
- Balawi (1166) described an inner ramp roofed with masonry, wide enough for two horsemen to pass at once. — https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pharos
- Light produced by a furnace at the top; fire lit at night; later Arab sources describe a mirror, presumably polished bronze (uncertain, so the script says "later writers say"). Fuel likely oil (wood scarce). — https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pharos ; https://www.worldhistory.org/Lighthouse_of_Alexandria/
- A statue of Zeus (or Poseidon) on top; Zeus "mostly agreed upon". — https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pharos ; https://www.worldhistory.org/Lighthouse_of_Alexandria/
- Height at least 100 m (estimates 100–140 m); one of the world's tallest man-made structures for many centuries. — https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pharos ; https://www.worldhistory.org/Lighthouse_of_Alexandria/
- Damaged by earthquakes 956–1303; the remnant disappeared in 1480 when Sultan Qaitbay built a fort using the fallen stone. — https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pharos
- "Pharos" became the word for lighthouse: French phare, Italian/Spanish faro, Portuguese farol. — https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pharos
