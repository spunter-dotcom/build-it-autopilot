# Sources: water clock (clepsydra)

- Wikipedia, "Water clock" — https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Water_clock
  - Karnak clepsydra, temple of Amun-Re, Amenhotep III, c. 1417–1379 BC; stone vessel with "sloping sides that allowed water to drip at a nearly constant rate from a small hole near the bottom."
  - "twelve separate columns with consistently spaced markings on the inside", one per month, "to allow for the variations of the seasonal hours."
  - Athenian court clepsydra: filled fully for important cases, partially for minor ones; the hole was stopped with wax during interruptions.
  - Ctesibius (died 222 BC) "invented an indicator system typical for later clocks such as the dial and pointer."
- Egypt Museum, "Clepsydra of Karnak" — https://egypt-museum.com/clepsydra-of-karnak/
  - Amenhotep III, ca. 1391–1353 BC; Egyptian alabaster (calcite); shaped "so that it falls at a uniform rate"; "a very small hole made in the center of the bottom"; "the value of each hour varied according to the season of the year."
- History of Information, Karnak clepsydra — https://historyofinformation.com/detail.php?entryid=3505 (c. 1417–1379 BC)
- History of Information, Ctesibius — https://historyofinformation.com/detail.php?entryid=2306
  - Circa 250 BCE, Alexandria. Of the speaking clepsydra: "A person was free to speak until the tube was empty." "As it emptied, the decrease in pressure slowed the dripping." "A float and pointer set in a third container indicated the time elapsed"; a large body of water kept the clepsydra full.
- MacTutor (St Andrews), "Timekeeping in the Ancient World: Water-clocks" — https://mathshistory.st-andrews.ac.uk/HistTopics/Water_clocks/
  - Judicial water clocks in common use in Athens around 430 B.C.; the type of speech decided how much water.
  - Inflow klepsydra "supplied from a large reservoir of water kept at a constant level", with "An overflow pipe ... near the top of the reservoir".
  - Vitruvius: the float is "an inverted bowl, which is called phellos"; "A bar is affixed to this float, with teeth on its other end that engage with similar teeth on a revolving drum."
