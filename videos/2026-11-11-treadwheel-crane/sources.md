| Claim in the script / caption | Source |
|---|---|
| Treadwheel cranes were key to building Gothic cathedrals | [W] "the treadwheel crane played a pivotal role in the construction of the lofty Gothic cathedrals." |
| Stones were lifted with cranes and pulleys | [C] "stones are lifted using cranes and pulleys." |
| A wheel turning a shaft, wide enough for two workers walking side by side | [W] "a large wooden wheel turning around a central shaft with a treadway wide enough for two workers walking side by side." |
| The load rises as the people inside walk ("hamster wheel") | [W] "The often heavy charge is lifted as the individual inside the treadwheel crane walks." [C] "a 'hamster wheel' which is large enough for two men." |
| Big wheel vs thin axle gives the lifting power | [W] the treadwheel "possesses a much bigger mechanical advantage due to its larger diameter"; a thinner shaft gives "a greater mechanical advantage." |
| Lewis: a dovetail hole cut in the top of the stone, three iron legs slotted in | [L] lewis "is inserted into a specially prepared hole, or seating, in the top of a stone"; three-legged lewis "fits into a dovetailed seating in the top of a building stone"; "the three legs thus form a dovetail shape"; the inner leg goes in last, "pushing the outer legs into contact with the inside of the lewis hole." [W] ashlar blocks were lifted "by sling, lewis hole or devil's clamp". |
| Most cranes had no brake; the wheel's friction held it | [W] "medieval cranes rarely featured ratchets or brakes to forestall the load from running backward"; "the high friction force exercised by medieval treadwheels, which normally prevented the wheel from accelerating beyond control." |
| A crane master steers the load sideways with a small rope | [W] the crane master "was able to manipulate the movement laterally by a small rope attached to the load." |
| The crane was rebuilt on the walls and moved up as the building grew | [W] once a floor was done the crane was "reassembled on the roof beams from where it was moved from bay to bay during construction of the vaults"; "the crane 'grew' and 'wandered' with the building." |
| Cranes left in church towers, above the vault, under the roof | [W] "all extant construction cranes in England are found in church towers above the vaulting and below the roof". |
| Canterbury: a treadwheel still used until the 1970s | [C] the original treadwheel in Bell Harry Tower "still remains"; "this medieval 'crane' was still used up until the 1970s when it failed to pass modern Health and Safety tests." |
| Caption: earliest reference about 1225, in France | [W] earliest reference in France "about 1225." |
| Caption: Roman version with a treadwheel lifted about 6,000 kg | [W] "In case the winch has been replaced by a treadwheel, the maximum load even doubled to 6000 kg at only half the crew." |
| Caption: Bell Harry Tower completed in 1498 | [C] Bell Harry Tower "was completed in 1498." |
